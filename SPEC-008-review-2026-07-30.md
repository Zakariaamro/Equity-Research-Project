# SPEC-008 — First visual review

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Reviewed:** commit `271e605`, running locally at `localhost:8502`
**Status:** Findings only. Nothing here is designed — SPEC-008 v1.3 (or SPEC-009) decides what is taken.

The dashboard runs, all four pages render, and the two interactions AppTest could not
verify both work: **Plotly click opens the evidence panel** (confirmed on Metrics for both
AMZN and MU, showing formula and raw inputs), and **both toggles behave sensibly**. AC6 is
satisfied in the real browser, not just in simulation.

What follows is everything wrong with it, split into defects (the display is
misrepresenting or losing information) and requested changes (the design is working as
specified, and the specification should change).

---

## Part 1 — Defects

These are bugs. They are ordered by severity.

### D1 — Dollar amounts are being destroyed by LaTeX rendering

**Severity: critical.** This corrupts the single most important class of content on a
financial dashboard.

Rendered on Amazon's Overview:

> [grouping] Amazon disclosed multiple large new funding commitments to AI-related
> counterparties in the period, including a further
> *35.0billionequitycommitmenttopurchaseOpenAISeriesCPreferredStockandasubsequent−event*20.0
> billion financing facility for Anthropic.

And on Micron's:

> A federal jury found Micron's memory modules infringe two Netlist patents and awarded
> *425millionand*20 million in damages, for a $445 million total verdict.

**Cause:** Streamlit's markdown renders `$...$` as inline LaTeX. Any sentence containing
two dollar figures has everything between them swallowed into math mode — which is why the
spaces vanish and the font turns to italic serif. The tell is the stripped whitespace.

**Fix:** escape `$` as `\$` in every string that reaches `st.markdown`/`st.write` and can
contain currency — brief sentences, finding quotes, observation statements, evidence lines.
This belongs in `dashboard/format.py` as a single escaping helper applied at the display
boundary, not sprinkled at call sites, and not by editing the stored text.

**Test:** `test_currency_in_narrative_text_is_escaped` — a sentence containing two `$`
figures survives rendering with both amounts intact and no math-mode markup.

Worth noting how this got through: every one of the 18 briefs is stored correctly in the
database. The corruption happens only at render. AppTest asserts on the *values* passed to
Streamlit elements, so the strings it checked were the correct ones — the defect lives
entirely in what the browser does with them afterwards. This is the second finding in two
days that only a real browser could produce.

### D2 — The company name is missing everywhere

Every Overview header reads `(MU)` or `(AMZN)` — an empty string, a space, then the ticker
in parentheses.

`companies.name` is populated correctly in the database (`Micron Technology, Inc.`,
`Amazon.com, Inc.`, `NVIDIA Corporation`), so this is a display-layer bug: the name is
either not being selected or not being passed through.

### D3 — AC9's "reason" is unreachable, so AC9 is only nominally met

Micron's Overview shows three metrics as `Not available …` — truncated with an ellipsis,
with no way to read the reason. Zakaria hit this directly: *"Micron's overview does have a
few 'not available' but I can't access the reason."*

AC9 requires a NULL metric to render "with its reason." Rendering a *clipped* reason that
cannot be expanded satisfies the letter and not the point. `st.metric` truncates its value
string to the column width.

**Fix:** the reason needs somewhere it actually fits — a caption line beneath the metric, a
tooltip via `st.metric(help=...)`, or an expander. Any of the three; the requirement is
that a reader can read it.

**Test:** `test_null_metric_reason_is_reachable` — assert the full reason string is present
in the rendered output, not a truncated prefix.

### D4 — Free cash flow is displayed with no unit

Amazon's "Where is the cash going?" row reads:

| Capex to revenue | Capex to depreciation | Free cash flow | Cash conversion cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24.4% | 3.44x | **-18,171** | -51 days |

Three of the four carry units. The fourth is a bare number that could be thousands,
millions, or billions. AC3b claims USD renders in millions with a `$m` label; that is not
happening here.

`METRIC_REGISTRY` already carries `unit` and `precision` per metric, so the capability
exists and is simply not being applied on this tile. Check whether other USD-denominated
metrics have the same gap rather than fixing this one instance.

### D5 — Raw metric identifiers leak into prose

`gross_margin`, `operating_margin`, `revenue_yoy`, `revenue_qoq`, `asset_turnover`,
`fixed_asset_turnover`, `ebitda_margin`, `net_margin`, `roe` all appear as snake_case
internal names in the evidence lines and in "What changed?".

The registry has `display_name` for all 45 metrics. It is not being used in the observation
statements.

This is a small fix with a disproportionate effect on whether the page reads as a finished
product or as a debug view.

### D6 — Number formatting is inconsistent between briefs

Amazon: *"Gross margin of 51.82% was the highest level seen in the trailing 18 quarters."*

Micron: *"Gross margin reached 0.8456, its highest level in 21 quarters."*

Same metric, same page type, one page apart — one as a percentage, one as a raw ratio.
The difference is that the generator writes the prose, so formatting varies run to run.

The registry knows `gross_margin` is a percentage with a set precision. Either the
generator prompt must require registry-consistent formatting, or — better, because it
cannot drift — numbers in brief sentences should be formatted by code at render time rather
than trusted from the model's output.

### D7 — Beneish M-score is meaningless to the reader it was written for

`-2.75` and `-2.61` are displayed bare under "Should I be suspicious?".

SPEC-008 requires the dashboard not intimidate a non-specialist. A reader who does not
already know the Beneish threshold (**-1.78**; below it is unremarkable, above it warrants
attention) learns nothing from `-2.75`, and cannot tell whether it is good or bad news.

This is not a request to interpret the number — it is a request to show the threshold it is
measured against. `-2.75 (threshold -1.78)` would be enough. The registry's
`higher_is_better` and `plausible_range` fields suggest the machinery for this already
half-exists.

### D8 — Mixed as-of dates sit in the same row without visual separation

Micron's cash row: three tiles say `as of May 28, 2026`, the fourth says
`FY as of Aug 28, 2025` — nine months older, in the same visual group, at the same type
size and weight. It is labelled, so it is not wrong; it is easy to misread as comparable.

### D9 — Chart axes are unitless ratios

The sparklines under "Is the business getting better or worse?" plot gross margin on an
axis reading `0.4`, `0.45`, `0.5` while the metric tiles for the same quantity read
`51.82%`. The same registry `unit` that fixes D4 should drive axis formatting.

### D10 — Structural duplication between "The brief" and "What changed?"

These lines appear in both sections, verbatim:

- `High — gross_margin of 0.8456 is the highest in 21 quarters.`
- `High — operating_margin of 0.8037 is the highest in 21 quarters.`

Zakaria flagged the repetition *within* the brief (see C2 below). The same content is also
repeated *between* the brief and the section beneath it. Whatever resolves C2 should resolve
this at the same time — the two are one problem seen at two scales.

## Checked and not a defect

Micron's Q3 FY2026 figures look wrong at first read — revenue of **$41.5B against $9.3B a
year earlier (+346%)**, gross margin **84.6%**, operating margin **80.4%**. I traced them to
source before flagging them, and they reconcile exactly:

| Period | Revenue | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY26 (90d) | 13.643B | 7.646B |
| Q2 FY26 (90d) | 23.860B | 17.755B |
| Q3 FY26 (90d) | 41.456B | 35.056B |
| **Sum** | **78.959B** | **60.457B** |
| YTD 9-month fact (272d) | 78.959B | 60.457B |

Three independently-tagged quarters sum to the separately-filed nine-month figure to the
dollar. That is precisely the reconciliation that would fail under a duration mismatch — the
classic XBRL error where a YTD fact is mistaken for a quarterly one. It does not fail.

So the pipeline is reading Micron correctly and these are the filed numbers. I flag it only
because **the dashboard gives a reader no way to reach that conclusion from the Overview
page.** A gross margin of 84.6% for a company that owns fabs is arresting enough that the
natural first reaction is "this is broken." The evidence panel answers it — but only on the
Metrics page, and only for someone who thinks to go looking. Consider whether headline
figures that sit outside a metric's `plausible_range` should surface their evidence trail
where they are shown, rather than one page away.

## Part 2 — Requested changes

Working as specified. The specification is what should change.

### C1 — Visual design: darker, textured, deliberate

Requested: *"a bit more towards the dark side with dark notes, but some more visual design.
Some cool patterns or some background shades, make it a little artsy, not like a kid drawing
something."*

**This appears to conflict with R8 and AC3c, and does not.** The existing rule is worth
restating precisely before anything is changed, because the reasoning behind it is sound and
must survive:

> No severity colour coding. Severity is conveyed by ordering and by an explicit text
> label, never by colour. Colour directs the eye before the brain reads anything.

That rule governs **colour as a carrier of analytical meaning**. It says nothing about the
chrome. Background shading, panel surfaces, type hierarchy, rules and spacing, texture — none
of these encode a claim about the data, and all of them are currently absent, which is why
the page reads as a default rather than a design.

**The line to hold:** no colour, weight, or emphasis may vary *as a function of the data*.
A high-severity observation and a low-severity one must look identical. Within that
constraint, the surface is free.

Concretely, and worth doing before anything more elaborate: the page is currently pure
default-dark with white text and no surfaces. Panels for the metric tiles and evidence
blocks, a background one step off pure black, a real type scale, and restrained use of the
existing accent already present in the sidebar chips would carry most of the distance.

### C2 — Strip the type prefixes; move evidence behind a click

Requested: remove the `[restatement]` / `[juxtaposition]` / `[grouping]` prefixes, and
collapse the `— High: …` evidence lines so the brief reads as prose, with evidence available
on click.

This is right, and it is worth being clear that it **is a change to AC7**, which currently
reads: *"Every brief sentence displays its sources adjacent, not behind a click."*

The reasoning for AC7 was that hiding a source makes it easy to stop checking. The reasoning
against it, now visible on a real page, is that the sources are currently *longer than the
sentences they support* and mostly restate them — so the brief does not read as a brief.
The Amazon Overview needs four screens before it reaches its first chart.

Whichever way this is resolved, resolve it deliberately and record it in the decision log —
this is exactly the kind of constraint that gets quietly dropped for convenience and then
cannot be re-argued later. A defensible middle: sources collapse behind a click, but the
**count** stays visible and unclickable (`3 sources`), so a sourceless sentence is
conspicuous without the sources being in the way.

The sentence types are still worth keeping in the database. They are how SPEC-007's R4
verification is dispatched, and the 70/19/10 distribution in ROADMAP-V2 is measured from
them. Only the display prefix goes.

### C3 — The five questions become an accordion

Requested: the questions listed one under another, each expanding on click, each
independently open or closed, state surviving navigation to the next question.

Note the interaction with C2: if the brief collapses, the Overview becomes short enough that
an accordion may be unnecessary. Worth building C2 first and re-reading the page before
committing to C3.

### C4 — Financials: a Bloomberg-style statement table

Requested:

- Line items as rows, periods as columns, oldest to newest left to right.
- Annual / quarterly toggle.
- A growth toggle that adds a **period-over-period % change beneath each figure** — not
  replacing it — in a dimmer shade and italic, so the reported number and the derived
  number are never confused.
- Sub-tabs for **Income statement**, **Balance sheet**, **Cash flow statement** rather than
  all three stacked on one page.

Two things this must not break. First, R8's display discipline: the growth row is a derived
number and belongs in the data layer, not computed inline in the page. Second, the italic
styling is doing real work here — it is the only thing separating a figure Micron filed from
a figure this project calculated, and that distinction is the entire premise of the project.
It should be legible at a glance and it should be documented as load-bearing, not
decorative.

### C5 — Sub-tab bar above the content, main nav stays left

Requested: main sections stay in the left panel; sub-sections appear as a horizontal bar
directly above the content area.

This is one shared component used by both C4 and C6, not two implementations. Build it once.

### C6 — Metrics: sub-tabs by category

Requested, in this order: Growth, Margins, Returns, Capital & cash, Working capital,
Solvency, Quality.

**These are already the registry's own categories, in the registry's own order:**

```
['growth', 'margins', 'returns', 'capital_cash', 'working_capital', 'solvency', 'quality']
```

So the tabs must be **derived from `METRIC_REGISTRY`, never hardcoded.** A hardcoded list of
seven would silently break AC5 — adding a metric in a new category would leave it with
nowhere to appear, and the failure would be invisible rather than loud. Deriving them keeps
the existing acceptance criterion true for free, and the display names for the tabs should
come from the registry too rather than being retyped here.

**Test:** extend the existing AC5 demonstration — a throwaway metric added under a *new*
category should produce a new tab with no page-file changes.

## Suggested order

1. **D1** — currency corruption. Nothing else matters while dollar figures are unreadable.
2. **D2, D4, D5, D6, D9** — one pass over the display layer. All five are the registry's
   `display_name`/`unit`/`precision` not being applied; they are close to one fix.
3. **D3, D7, D8, D10** — the remaining display defects.
4. **C2** — decide AC7 explicitly, log the decision, then implement. Re-read the Overview
   before touching C3.
5. **C5** — the shared sub-tab component.
6. **C4, C6** — the two pages that consume it.
7. **C1** — the visual pass, last, over a layout that has stopped moving.

C1 is deliberately last. Styling a layout that is still changing is work done twice.
